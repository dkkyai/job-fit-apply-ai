## Resume Tailoring Rules

1. **Match language to the JD.** If the JD says "test infrastructure" use that exact phrase. If it says "quality engineering" mirror it. Applicant tracking systems (ATS) match keywords literally.

2. **Reorder, don't fabricate.** Bring the most JD-relevant bullets to the top of each role. Never invent experience. If a skill is a gap, omit it — don't stretch.

3. **Quantify everything possible.** Concrete numbers — "$1K/month infra savings", "reduced CI flakiness from 18% to 4%", "3 platform engineers onboarded" — beat generic adjectives every time.

4. **Summary section must be customised.** Rewrite the professional summary to speak directly to this company and role. 3 sentences maximum.

5. **Skills section order matters.** List the most JD-relevant skills first. Drop irrelevant skills to avoid dilution.

6. **Keep it to 4 pages or less.** Senior/Staff-level roles can take the most space; entry-level roles should be tighter.

---

## Cover Letter Rules (only write if requested)

1. **Opening:** Name the specific role and company. Show you know what they do — one specific sentence about their product or mission.

2. **Middle (2 paragraphs):** Connect the candidate's two strongest matching experiences directly to the JD's core requirements. Cite specific projects by name for credibility.

3. **Close:** Confident, not begging. Express genuine interest and propose a next step.

4. **Tone:** Direct, senior-engineer-to-peer. Not formal corporate. Not casual. Think: how a respected staff engineer writes a Slack message to a VP.

5. **Length:** 3–4 short paragraphs. Under 350 words.

---

## Critical Constraints

- Do NOT mention skills the candidate doesn't have, even if the JD requests them
- Do NOT use phrases like "passionate about", "results-driven", "team player", "dynamic", "synergy"
- Do NOT pad with generic responsibilities — every bullet should show impact or scope
- Preserve all truthful career dates and company names exactly
